Christian journal prompts for women

These thirty prompts are for a quiet page and an honest prayer. Use one a day in a prayer journal for women, or write a few at the end of the week. There is no right length. A sentence is enough.

They are free to copy into your own notebook. If you would like lined pages, Scripture and a little art already printed for you, the journals further down are the ones these prompts were written to sit beside.

Prayer journal prompts

  1. Where did I notice God in an ordinary moment today?
  2. What am I carrying that I can place in God’s hands this morning?
  3. Who needs a quiet prayer from me this week, and what would I ask for them?
  4. Which worry keeps returning, and what would it look like to trust God with it?
  5. When did I last feel peace, and what was I doing?
  6. What do I need to tell God honestly, without shaming myself?
  7. What has God already carried me through that I can thank him for?
  8. Where do I need wisdom, rather than a quick answer?
  9. What would resting with God look like in the next hour?
  10. Which promise from Scripture do I need to read again today, and why?

Gratitude prompts

  1. Three small mercies from yesterday I do not want to forget.
  2. A person who showed me kindness, and how I might thank them.
  3. A part of my health or my body I can thank God for today.
  4. A meal, a chair, or a safe place I usually overlook.
  5. Something in creation that slowed me down recently.
  6. A hard day that still held one gift.
  7. A skill or task that lets me care for someone else.
  8. A verse, song or prayer that stayed with me this week.
  9. Something I can share, even if it feels small.
  10. If I wrote only one sentence of thanks tonight, what would it be?

Scripture reflection prompts

Read a short passage first — a psalm, a word of Jesus, or a verse from Isaiah — then answer one question.

  1. Copy the verse, then write one sentence about what it says to me today.
  2. What does this passage show me about God’s character?
  3. Is there a word here I want to understand better?
  4. How does this verse meet fear, love or patience in my real week?
  5. What would obedience look like in one small action?
  6. Who in the passage do I recognise, and why?
  7. What can I praise God for in these lines?
  8. Rewrite the verse as a short prayer in my own words.
  9. What question does this passage leave me asking God?
  10. How could I share this verse, kindly, with one other person?
